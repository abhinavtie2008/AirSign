02 The gesture set

Four moves.
The whole web.

Nothing to memorise. Every gesture mirrors something you already do with a mouse — just with a little more air in the room.

01 POINT

Point to move

Your index fingertip is the cursor. One Euro smoothing keeps it steady when you hold still and responsive when you move fast.

02 PINCH

Pinch to click

Touch thumb and index together. AirSign freezes your aim, clicks once, and highlights what it's about to hit before you commit.

= left click
03 FIST

Close your hand over a card, a slider, or any draggable thing. Your palm becomes the grip and the cursor follows it until you let go.

= click + hold + drag

Raise your index and middle fingers, then move your hand up or down. Distance is measured in hand widths, so camera distance doesn't change your speed.

03 Under the hood

Fast enough to feel native.
Private by design.

MediaPipe reads 21 landmarks per frame. AirSign turns them into a cursor and four gestures, then dispatches real pointer events to the page. Photons in, pixels out — the whole loop stays inside your browser.

  • ✓
    No frames leave the tab

    Tracking uses WebGL or WASM locally. After the model loads, no network requests are needed.

  • ✓
    No recording, ever

    Frames are read into a canvas and discarded. AirSign never stores an image or a landmark.

  • ✓
    You hold the off switch

    The camera is requested only when you start, and released the moment you press Stop.

04 Built in

The details that
make it usable.

Touchless control only works if it feels dependable. These are the small decisions that make AirSign feel less like a demo and more like an input device.

Privacy first

Your camera is not a cloud service.

No recordings, no snapshots, no frame analytics. The model ships with the extension and runs locally; the only network request is the one that loaded this page.

0 Buploaded

Side panel or bubble

Keep the full control panel open, or collapse it into a draggable camera bubble that floats over any page and survives navigation.

Click assist

A pinpoint cursor with a forgiving nature. Nearby buttons get highlighted, and a near miss lands on what you actually meant.

Distance-independent scroll

Scroll travel is measured in hand widths, not pixels, so the speed stays the same near or far from the lens.

Yours to tune

Reach, smoothing, mirror and pinch calibration. If the default feels off, the side panel can calibrate to your hand.

Any normal tab

News, docs, dashboards, your own projects. AirSign works on ordinary web pages — not just a walled-garden demo.

05 Install

One download.
Then it lives in Chrome.

AirSign 2.3 is a local Manifest V3 extension. The hand-tracking model is bundled, so the installer is big but the setup is small: unzip, load once, point forever.

  1. 01
    Download the build

    Grab AirSign-v2.3.0.zip from the latest GitHub release and unzip it somewhere you'll remember.

  2. 02
    Open your extensions

    Visit chrome://extensions and switch on Developer mode in the top right.

  3. 03
    Load unpacked

    Choose Load unpacked and select the unzipped folder — the one containing manifest.json.

  4. 04
    Point at something

    Click the AirSign icon, allow camera access, and start with your hand. Minimize the panel into a bubble whenever you want the screen back.

Chrome or Edge 116+ on desktop recommended. Camera permission is requested on first run and can be revoked at any time. Not on the Chrome Web Store yet — unpacked is the supported install for 2.3.

06 Questions

Good to know.

Still curious? The README has the architecture, the calibration details, and every known limitation.

Open the README →
Is my camera feed uploaded anywhere?+

No. Both the demo and the extension run MediaPipe's hand model locally using WebGL or WASM. Frames are drawn to a canvas, analysed, and immediately discarded. There is no image upload, no recording buffer, and no analytics on video.

Do I need to install anything for the playground?

No. This page loads the same vision model and gesture engine as the extension, directly from GitHub Pages. You only need a camera, a modern browser, and permission. The extension adds the side panel, camera bubble, settings, and control over other tabs.

Which browsers work?

The extension targets Chrome 116+ (and Chromium browsers like Edge). The demo works in current Chrome, Edge, and Firefox where camera access and WebAssembly are available; desktop is strongly recommended because hand tracking needs a steady camera and a reasonably large screen.

Why isn't my hand being detected?

Most misses are lighting or framing. Face a light source, keep your hand fully inside the camera view, and use one hand. Glasses, dark sleeves, and bright windows behind you can all make landmarks less stable. Try the Compact reach preset if you run out of room.

Does it work on every website?

On ordinary http and https pages, yes. Chrome blocks extensions from injecting scripts into browser-internal pages such as chrome://settings, the Chrome Web Store, and some PDF viewers. AirSign also needs the page to use standard pointer events, which covers the vast majority of the web.

How do I stop the camera?

Press Stop in the camera bubble or the side panel. The stream is released immediately and the Chrome camera indicator turns off. You can also revoke camera permission from the site or extension settings at any time.

What gets stored?

The extension stores your tuning preferences and bubble position locally in Chrome. This demo stores the same preferences and its bubble position in localStorage. No hand landmarks, images, or browsing history are saved.

Your camera is not a cloud service.

AirSign exists to make browsing easier, not to collect anything. There is no account, no telemetry, and no server that ever sees your hand. The model is in the box; your camera feed stays in the tab.
